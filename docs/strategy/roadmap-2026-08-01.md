# Rip Packs City — Roadmap, 2026-08-01

**Supersedes** `docs/strategy/roadmap-2026-07-18.md`. That doc was written the day after go-live and correctly said "the gate that matters now is traction, not features." Two weeks later the traction number has not moved at all, and today's audit found the reason is not mysterious. This roadmap keeps that gate and adds Trevor's new product direction — feature parity, the Hobby axis, and the read-only conversion — sequenced behind it.

## 2. The one metric that matters, and what would falsify the strategy

**Metric: weekly active users, where "active" means a human session that fires ≥2 events, one of which is a wallet paste or a saved-wallet read.** Not pageviews. Not sessions. Not Google impressions. The bar stays where it has been: **50 WAU unlocks the monetization conversation; nothing before it does.**

**Secondary metric, and the leading indicator: wallet-paste conversion on pages that have a wallet box.** ⚠ **Do not read this as a rate until the denominator is real.** Measured 2026-08-01: the honest denominator is ~8 human sessions, so 0/441 is a *traffic* reading, not a conversion reading. Track the ABSOLUTE count instead — `wallet_paste` is 25 lifetime / 4 in 30d / 1 in 7d. Any week with a paste from a session that is not Trevor's is new information.

**What would falsify the current strategy — i.e. what would tell us the problem is NOT distribution:**

1. We drive 500 real (referrer-bearing, multi-event) sessions to `/insights/account-value` and paste conversion stays under ~3%. That would mean the wedge itself does not land, and the answer is a different product, not a bigger megaphone.
2. Pastes happen but nobody returns — a second session within 7 days under ~10%. That would mean the answer ("here is what it's worth") is a one-time curiosity, not a habit, and the roadmap should pivot hard toward alerting/watching rather than valuation.
3. Someone else ships the dual-scale multi-collection valuation first and it does not move their numbers either. That would say the wedge is not a wedge.

Write these down now so we cannot retroactively decide we were right.

## 3. Phase 0 — Distribution & Activation (this week)

Small, concrete, high-leverage. Nothing here is a feature. Everything here is grounded in what today's audit actually measured.

### 0.1 The account has no job — give it one

Right now everything on the site is free and anonymous. Signing up buys the user **a bookmark**. That is the whole value proposition of an RPC account today, and it explains 0 signups in 45 days better than any funnel theory.

The fix is not to gate content (that was tried; the un-gate on 07-17 was correct). The fix is to make the account **watch the market while the user sleeps**:

- Save a wallet → get a weekly (or on-threshold) "your collection moved $X" email. The `weekly-digest` send-loop already exists and is tested. `email_subscribers` is 0 because nothing ever asks.
- Save a wallet → get an alert when an edition you own crosses a floor/FMV threshold. `manage_alerts` and `manage_deal_subscriptions` are already concierge tools.
- The pitch on the signup CTA stops being "create an account" and becomes **"we'll tell you when your collection changes."**

This is the single highest-leverage item on this list. It converts a read-only lookup tool into something with a reason to exist between visits.

### 0.2 `/insights/account-value` is buried at card #24 of 26

It is, by today's measurement, the best-optimized page in the product — and it is the direct answer to the question the whole company is built around. It sits 24th out of 26 cards on the `/insights` hub.

- Promote it to card #1 on `/insights`.
- Link it from the homepage above the fold.
- Give it a distinct, searchable URL story (see 0.3).

### 0.3 The homepage `<h1>` is the brand name, not the question

`<h1>Rip Packs City</h1>` optimizes for people who already know we exist. There are 39 of those, lifetime, from Google.

Change the h1 to the **question people actually type**: something in the shape of *"What is your NBA Top Shot collection worth?"* — with the wallet box directly underneath it, above the fold, on the homepage. Brand goes in the logo and the `<title>`, not the h1.

This is a ~20 line change with a months-long SEO tail. It is the cheapest thing on this list.

### 0.4 ~~Fix the wallet-box leak (441 views → 0 pastes)~~ — CLOSED BY MEASUREMENT (2026-08-01)

**Do not do this work.** The step-1 measurement below was run and the denominator collapsed: ~8 real human sessions, not 441. The box is fine; nothing arrived to use it. Retained below only so the reasoning is not re-derived by a future session.

Before driving traffic, understand why the highest-intent control converts at zero. Candidate causes, in order of my suspicion:

1. **The box does not say what happens next.** "Enter wallet" with no promise. It should say what it returns and that it takes one click and no signup.
2. **Placement.** If it is below the fold or below a dense board, a scanning visitor never reaches it.
3. **Address friction.** Most Top Shot collectors know their *username*, not their `0x…`. If the box only accepts an address, most people literally cannot use it. Username resolution exists (75% resolution rate) — it must be the primary affordance, not a fallback.
4. **Crawler-dominated denominator.** If 96.7% of sessions have no referrer, most of those 441 views may be bots and the real human denominator is tiny. **Measure this first** — split the view count by referrer-present before concluding anything. A design change chasing a bot number is wasted work.

Do (4) first. It is a query, not a code change, and it decides whether 1–3 are urgent.

### 0.5 One distribution channel, deliberately, with a pre-declared success bar

The standing rule is no promo until Trevor says so — that rule stands and this does not override it. But when it happens, it should be **one channel, one message, one pre-declared bar**, not a scattershot launch:

- The message is the wedge: *"see what your whole collection is worth — every collection, every chain, in one page."*
- The bar is declared before posting (e.g. 100 referrer-bearing sessions, ≥5 wallet pastes). Below the bar → the message or the channel is wrong; iterate, don't repeat.
- Instrument it with a `?ref=` so it is distinguishable from the 96.7% crawler noise.

**Everything in Phase 1 onward is worth less than Phase 0 until Phase 0 produces a non-zero number.**

## 4. Phase 1 — The dual-scale "what is my collection worth" promise

This is the wedge, and it is worth being precise about what makes it defensible: **it is not valuation, it is dual-scale valuation.** Plenty of tools tell you what one moment is worth. A few tell you what one wallet in one collection is worth. Nobody answers both of these at once:

- **(a) Everything, everywhere** — across all collections and all chains, in one number, with the breakdown.
- **(b) Inside one collection** — across its items (moments/pins/cards) *and* its packs, which is the part everybody skips because sealed-pack valuation is hard.

The packs half of (b) is the genuinely differentiated piece. It is also the half most likely to be quietly wrong if we are not disciplined about coverage — which is why honest disclosure is a Phase-1 requirement, not a Phase-3 polish item.

### What has to be true

1. **Per-collection wallet valuation covering BOTH items and packs.** Items are largely done. Packs are partially done and unevenly — a wallet holding 12 sealed UFC packs has no EV to value them with, because there are zero UFC pack distributions in the database.
2. **A cross-collection, cross-chain rollup** that sums (1) honestly, does not silently drop a collection it cannot price, and shows the user *what it could not price*.
3. **Coverage disclosure everywhere the data is thin.** The pattern already exists and works: Panini's `meta.coverage` "treat this as a floor, not a census" banner, and today's "priced from asks" disclosure across four surfaces. Apply the same discipline to the rollup. A total that silently omits 60% of a Panini collection is worse than no total.

### What the data can actually support today

**FMV coverage (share of editions with any FMV):**

| Collection | FMV coverage | Caveat that must be disclosed |
|---|---|---|
| NBA Top Shot | 99.3% | Best-supported. But 9.2% of *liquid* editions (409/4,443) carry FMV >2× their own sales median — concentrated in the ask-blended LOW tier. See §8. |
| NFL All Day | 100% | Sunset collection; finite catalog. Market is alive. |
| LaLiga Golazos | 100% | Extremely thin trading volume — a "100%" coverage number over a market that traded 6 moments in 4 days is a coverage figure, not a confidence figure. |
| UFC Strike | 89.8% coverage, **only 28.6% priced** | Market effectively dead since 05-13 (Aptos migration). Values are historical. Must be labelled as such. |
| Disney Pinnacle | 100% | Render-keyed, separate data plane (`pinnacle_fmv_history`), not `fmv_snapshots`. |
| Candy MLB | 100% | Young market. Many editions priced off 1–2 sales. |
| Panini | **39.6%** | Listing-gated discovery — an edition only enters the index once it is *listed*. This is a floor, not a census, and the disclosure is already built into the surface. Do not remove it. |

**Pack EV coverage:** exists for Top Shot, All Day, Golazos, Pinnacle, and Candy. **Zero UFC pack distributions exist.** So "your packs are worth $X" is answerable for 5 of 7 collections and structurally unanswerable for UFC today.

**Ask-derived FMV, now disclosed (today's ship):** 5,794 Flow editions and 727 Panini editions are priced at 0.9× a *single* ask. That is 34.5% of Panini's headline "value sealed in packs." This is the exact class of number that makes a rollup look authoritative and be wrong. It is now labelled. Keep it labelled.

### Phase 1 deliverable

One page — the promoted `/insights/account-value` — that takes a wallet (or several, per Phase 4) and returns:

```
Your collection:            $X,XXX
  NBA Top Shot   items $A + packs $B
  NFL All Day    items $C + packs $D
  Disney Pinnacle items $E + packs $F
  …
  Not priced: 214 Panini cards (listing-gated), 12 UFC packs (no EV model)
```

The "Not priced" line is not a weakness to hide. It is the thing that makes the rest of the number trustworthy, and it is the sentence no competitor writes.

## 5. Phase 2 — Feature parity + the IA restructure

Trevor's structure, per collection:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **My Wallet** | Items (Moments/Pins/Cards) + Packs → each with Own / Sold / Activity History |
| **Market** | Items + Packs, **edition level** |
| **Sniper** | Items + Packs, **serial/individual level**, deals on most-recently-listed |
| **Play** | Challenges, Fast Break, Hardcourt.io, etc. |
| **Sets** | — |
| **Analytics** | **Overview folds in here — Overview is redundant and gets deleted** |

Folding Overview into Analytics is correct and overdue. Overview is a landing page for a tab bar, which is a navigation artifact, not a user need. Deleting it removes a route, a tab, and a maintenance surface, and it removes the "where do I click" ambiguity between two dashboards that show overlapping numbers.

**Framing discipline:** parity is not a feature-count exercise. Parity is what makes the Phase-1 promise true in every collection instead of just Top Shot. Where a collection genuinely cannot support a section, the answer is to say so on the page — not to ship an empty tab.

### Parity matrix

**Ready** = data exists and a surface exists or is a straightforward port. **Partial** = data exists but is thin/gated/needs disclosure. **Not possible** = no upstream data source exists today.

| Section | Top Shot | All Day | Golazos | UFC | Pinnacle | Candy | Panini |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **My Wallet — items** | Ready | Ready | Ready | Ready | Partial — separate data plane (`pinnacle_editions`, render-keyed); `scan-pinnacle-wallet` edge fn has written **nothing** to `wallet_moments_cache` since 2026-06-10 (fix committed, never deployed — Handoff #3) | Partial — wmc = 25,375 = supply exactly, but username resolution is Flow-only so 0/46 Candy names resolve | **Not possible today** — no wallet-ownership index; discovery is listing-gated |
| **My Wallet — packs** | Ready | Ready | Partial | **Not possible** — 0 pack distributions | Partial | Partial — pack-EV model exists (`candy_pack_ev_model`) but the Rainbow leg is largely unpriced | Partial — pack state exists (`panini_pack_state`), no per-wallet ownership |
| **My Wallet — Own/Sold/Activity** | Ready (`get_wallet_pack_history`, `sold_any`) | Ready | Partial | Partial | Partial | Partial | Not possible |
| **Market (edition level)** | Ready | Ready (`get_allday_market_editions`) | Ready | Ready but a dead market — label it | Ready (render-keyed live-listings) | Ready (`candy_secondary_board`) | Partial — listing-gated, 39.6% |
| **Sniper (serial level)** | Ready | Ready | Ready | Partial — few live listings | Ready | Partial — ~15 asks; `candy_listings` live | Partial |
| **Market/Sniper — packs** | Ready | Ready | Partial | Not possible | Partial | Partial | Partial |
| **Play** | Ready (Challenges, Fast Break, RTR) | **Not possible** — no game layer exists upstream | Not possible | Not possible | Not possible | Not possible | Not possible |

**The honest reading of that matrix:** Play is a Top-Shot-only section and will stay that way — there is no upstream game layer on the other six. **Sequencing inside Phase 2:**
1. Fold Overview into Analytics (deletes a route; lowest risk, immediate IA win).
2. Bring the Packs half of My Wallet/Market/Sniper to parity where pack data exists — this is what Phase 1 depends on.
3. Deploy the `scan-pinnacle-wallet` fix (Handoff #3, one command) — Pinnacle wallet reads have been silently dead for 7 weeks.
4. Leave Panini My Wallet explicitly out of scope and say so on the surface.

## 6. Phase 3 — The Hobby axis + collection-agnostic Team Hub

Trevor's idea: a **second navigation axis** — Basketball, Football, UFC, Baseball, Comics, Pokémon — with the same six sections, and toggleable collection filters. He asked for this to be workshopped rather than restated, so here are three real options with their trade-offs.

**Data model note that applies to all three:** the precedent already exists. `chain` lives on `collections` only, is a Postgres enum (`chain_type`), and every dependent row reaches chain via the `collection_id` FK; `collection_chains` is the canonical join view. A `hobby` (or `sport`) dimension is the exact same shape: **`ALTER TYPE`-backed enum column on `collections`, plus a `collection_hobbies` join view** granted to anon/authenticated/service_role. Zero dependent-table changes. That part is ~1 hour of DB work and is not the hard part of any option below.

The hard part is IA and SEO.

### Option A — Hobby as a first-class route prefix: `/basketball/market`

Hobby becomes a peer of collection in the URL. `/basketball/market` aggregates Top Shot + (future basketball collections); `/nba-top-shot/market` still exists for the single-collection view.

- **SEO:** strongest. "basketball nft market", "baseball card collection value" are searchable phrases with volume; collection-branded slugs are not. This is the option that could actually move the 39-lifetime-Google-referrals number.
- **IA:** clean and honest — hobby genuinely is a peer dimension, not a filter.
- **Engineering cost:** highest. Every one of the six sections needs a hobby-scoped data path. Today almost every read is `WHERE collection_id = $1`; hobby needs `WHERE collection_id IN (…)`, which means auditing every RPC and view for single-collection assumptions. The chain-abstraction Phase-E audit found 85 "assumes-Flow" surfaces — expect a similar order of "assumes one collection."
- **Risk:** duplicate content. `/basketball/market` and `/nba-top-shot/market` show the same rows while basketball has exactly one collection. Needs a canonical strategy from day one (recommendation: canonical points at the hobby URL when the hobby has ≥2 collections, at the collection URL when it has 1).

### Option B — Hobby as a persistent filter/toggle over existing collection routes

Keep every route as-is. Add a hobby selector in the chrome that sets a multi-collection scope, carried as a URL param (`?hobby=basketball`), following the existing `?section=packs` precedent from the 07-18 IA reorg.

- **SEO:** weakest — query params do not generate indexable pages. Gains nothing on the discovery problem, which is the actual problem.
- **IA:** muddy. The tab bar already keys off collection; adding an orthogonal scope selector above it means two controls that both change what you're looking at, which is the classic "two navigations" confusion.
- **Engineering cost:** lowest by far. Reuses every existing route and layout. Could ship in days.
- **Risk:** low technical risk, high "we built it and it changed nothing" risk.

### Option C — A separate `/hobby/...` tree that is aggregate-only

`/hobby/basketball` exists as a **destination page**, not a parallel six-section app: one page per hobby that rolls up cross-collection value, top movers, and links out to each member collection's real tabs. No duplicated Market/Sniper/Sets under it.

- **SEO:** strong where it counts — one high-quality, genuinely distinct indexable page per hobby, targeting the searched phrase, with no duplicate-content problem because it does not duplicate anything.
- **IA:** clean. Hobby is a *lens*, collections remain the *workspace*. Users land on hobby, work in collection.
- **Engineering cost:** moderate — one new route family, one aggregate RPC per section-worth-of-rollup. Substantially less than A because there are no hobby-scoped Market/Sniper/Sets.
- **Risk:** does not deliver "the same six sections per hobby" as literally stated.

### Recommendation: **C now, A later, never B**

Ship **Option C** first. It captures ~80% of the SEO and positioning value of A for ~30% of the cost, it does not require auditing every RPC for single-collection assumptions, and — critically — it is the right shape for the thing we actually need right now, which is *indexable pages that answer a searched question*. `/hobby/basketball` with "what your basketball collection is worth across Top Shot and beyond" is a Phase-0 distribution asset disguised as a Phase-3 IA feature.

Then, **when a hobby actually has two or more collections with real data** (basketball does not yet — Top Shot is alone in it), promote to Option A for that hobby specifically. The `collection_hobbies` view means the data layer is already ready; only the routing and the multi-collection reads are new work, and by then there is a concrete reason to do them.

Do not ship B. A query-param filter over the existing routes buys nothing on the only axis that matters (discovery) and adds a second navigation control to a UI that just finished consolidating one.

### Collection-agnostic Team Hub

This is a natural child of the Hobby axis and is genuinely compelling: **`/team/portland-trail-blazers` showing Blazers assets across Top Shot *and* Panini in one view.** Nobody else can do this, because nobody else indexes both.

Two blockers, both known and both in the handoff:

1. **Slug vocabularies do not agree.** There are three non-interchangeable team-slug forms across the codebase, and Golazos accented team names were 404-ing until today's fix. A cross-collection team page needs a **canonical team registry** — one row per real-world team with the per-collection aliases hanging off it — before it can join Top Shot's `team_name` to Panini's.
2. **219 Top Shot player pages currently display the wrong current team** (Handoff #9) because there are 3,075 duplicate `players` rows and `get_player_detail` tie-breaks arbitrarily among up to 32 rows per slug. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander shows as a Clipper. **Fix that before building anything else on top of team/player identity** — a cross-collection Team Hub built on a broken team assignment is a more visible version of the same bug.

Sequence: fix #9 → build the canonical team registry → then Team Hub.

## 7. Phase 4 — Read-only conversion + the wallet/identity model

### 7.1 Strip every write/transactional surface

Trevor's call, and it is the right one. Rationale: low friction, low risk, and the underlying data is public on-chain anyway. Concretely:

- **Cart** — already shelved. `lib/cart/**` remains, `CartButton` has zero importers, but a `CartDrawer` was observed still rendering on a cached `/nba-top-shot/overview`. Shelved is not deleted. **Delete it.**
- **Trade Hub** — shelved, but on 2026-07-31 the 5 FCL submitters and 2 client sign helpers were **wired for real** and are **UNVERIFIED on-chain** (the contract is not deployed; the mandated Cadence-MCP mainnet verification could not run). Going read-only means this surface gets **deleted, not gated.** Leaving live-fire `fcl.mutate` code in the tree behind an env-var gate is exactly the configuration where a future env change ships an untested on-chain write.
- **Gifting** — live at `/dashboard/gift`, account-linked. Delete the surface.
- **Everything that remains** should be provably read-only. The check is mechanical: after the strip, there should be **zero `fcl.mutate` call sites** in the tree. Make that a test.

The payoff is not just risk reduction. It is that "we never touch your assets and we never ask for a transaction" becomes a **positioning statement** — and in a hobby full of people who have been rugged, that is a real trust asset, not a footnote.

### 7.2 Replace the `owner_key` identity model

Today's audit produced the most important structural finding of the day: **`owner_key` is polymorphic and client-controlled.** It can be an auth UUID, a `profile_bio.username`, or a `0x` address, and **there is no server-side mapping table.** That is why five routes were IDOR-able, and it is why any future feature keyed on "who is this" is building on sand.

Trevor's wallet model is the replacement, and it is a better model than what exists:

> Users enter the wallet usernames/addresses they want to track on their RPC profile — **one per blockchain**, except Flow, which allows **three** (Dapper, Flow Wallet, Flow EVM) once each is ready.

Concretely that is a real table:

```
user_wallets (
  user_id      uuid    not null references auth.users,
  chain        chain_type not null,
  wallet_kind  text    not null,   -- 'dapper' | 'flow_wallet' | 'flow_evm' | 'primary'
  address      text    not null,
  label        text,
  verified_at  timestamptz,
  primary key (user_id, chain, wallet_kind)
)
```

- The `(user_id, chain, wallet_kind)` PK **enforces the one-per-chain / three-for-Flow rule structurally**, not in application code.
- RLS: a user reads and writes only their own rows. Server-side writes resolve `user_id` from the **session**, never from a request body. That single rule is what kills the entire IDOR class permanently.
- Every existing `owner_key` consumer migrates to `user_id`. This is the debt-repayment item that unlocks safe personalization.

### 7.3 Sign-in: low friction, with honest trade-offs

| Option | Friction | Status | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Magic link (email)** | Low | **Live today**; front door open since 07-20 (allow-by-default minus `deny_list`) | Requires an email round-trip; ~20–30% of users abandon at the inbox switch. Still the right default. |
| **Listing challenge** (wallet proves ownership by listing a moment at a unique price) | High | **Built and live** | Genuinely proves wallet ownership. Too heavy for signup; correct as an *optional upgrade* for users who want a verified badge. |
| **Sign in with Dapper** | Lowest for Top Shot collectors | **Blocked** on Dapper developer access (request pending) | The single best option for our exact audience — Dapper custodies their accounts. Worth chasing; not worth blocking on. |
| **No account at all** (paste a wallet, get an answer) | Zero | **Live today** | This is the current state, and it converts at 0 signups because the account has no job. Phase 0.1 fixes the job, not the friction. |

**Recommendation:** magic link stays the default; add "paste a wallet → we'll email you when it moves → confirm your email" as the *signup path*, so the account is created as a side effect of getting something valuable rather than as a prerequisite.

### 7.4 "Public financials are fine, but X must never be exposed"

Trevor is right that wallet financials are not the risk — they are on-chain and already public. Being concrete about what *is* the risk, because today's audit found three of these live:

**Never exposed, non-negotiable:**

1. **Session tokens and credentials of any kind.** Today's P0 was live Dapper session cookies and an RS256 JWT containing a real email, legal name, and Flow account id, committed to a tracked file in a **public** repo. HEAD is cleaned; **git history still contains them** (Handoff #1, operator-only).
2. **Cost basis tied to an identity.** On-chain data says a wallet bought a moment for $40. It does **not** say who that wallet belongs to. `moment_acquisitions` (790,801 rows, 89,117 with a buy price, across 7,627 wallets) was **anon-readable** until today. Joined against a profile that names the wallet owner, that is a stranger's realized P&L with their name on it. Revoked today; keep it revoked.
3. **Email addresses**, and anything that maps an email to a wallet.
4. **Any write path to another user's data.** This is the IDOR class — five routes closed today, `lib/auth/owner-key-guard.ts` added. The permanent fix is §7.2: resolve identity from the session, never from a body parameter.
5. **Watchlists and alert configurations.** `watchlist_items` was anon-readable (revoked today). A watchlist is a statement of intent — it tells a competitor what a user is about to buy.

**The durable rule, learned the hard way twice in this codebase:** route-gating is not data-gating. `proxy.ts` gates HTTP routes; a table with the default Supabase `anon` grant is still readable at `/rest/v1/<table>` regardless. Every new table needs an explicit `REVOKE SELECT … FROM anon, authenticated`, verified with `has_table_privilege`, not by reading `information_schema`.

### 7.5 The concierge must be re-hardened for this model

`/api/support-chat` has 29 tools and today's model of identity. Under the multi-wallet, read-only model it needs:

- **No write tools.** `manage_alerts` / `manage_watchlist` / `manage_deal_subscriptions` must resolve the user from the session, not from anything the model can be talked into passing.
- **Multi-wallet awareness** — "what is my collection worth" should mean *all* the user's registered wallets across all chains, which is the Phase-1 promise expressed conversationally, and is arguably the best demo of the wedge we have.
- **Mirrored to Discord and Telegram.** This is a distribution item as much as a product one: a bot that answers "what's this moment worth" inside the Discord where collectors already live is a lower-friction front door than any webpage. It should be scoped in Phase 0/1, not deferred to Phase 4.

## 8. Known-correctness items that gate trust

These are in the handoff with full detail; flagged here because two of them undermine the Phase-1 promise directly:

- **9.2% of liquid Top Shot editions (409 of 4,443 with ≥4 sales/30d) carry an FMV more than 2× their own sales median**, concentrated in the ask-blended LOW confidence tier. On a page that says "your collection is worth $X," that is the number that gets us called wrong in public. **This is FMV pricing logic — hand off, do not autonomously retune.**
- **219 Top Shot player pages show the wrong current team.** Named examples: Shai Gilgeous-Alexander as a Clipper, Donovan Mitchell as a Jazz, Jalen Brunson as a Maverick. Any basketball fan who lands on one of those pages concludes the whole site is stale. This is the highest-visibility correctness bug in the product and it gates the Team Hub.
- **GitHub Actions is dropping 60–83% of sub-hourly ticks** (`topshot-listing-cache`: 72/day scheduled, 12/day actual, median gap 105 min against a 20-min design). Vercel crons and cron-job.org both run at ~100%. Move both affected workflows to Vercel crons.

## 9. Standing guardrails (carried forward, unchanged)

- **No paywall, no Stripe, no public launch push until 50+ WAU.**
- **No infra spend pre-revenue.** Levers first. The DB is the binding constraint and pipelines consume nearly all of it at WAU 0.
- **Verify pages by rendered DOM, not HTTP 200.** Streaming shells always return 200. The Playwright e2e smoke (35 routes, every 6h) exists for exactly this.
- **Ship direct to `main`.** No branches, no PRs.
- **Log every change touching `main` or prod state to `docs/overnight/ledger.md`** with a revert path, in the same turn it ships. Re-read the ledger from disk immediately before writing it — it is append-at-top and multiple sessions write concurrently.
- **Commit the ledger BEFORE the code** so the code commit is the tip and auto-deploys.

## 10. What I am deliberately NOT recommending, and why

**1. Do not build the Hobby axis as a full six-section parallel app (Option A) right now.**
Basketball currently contains exactly one collection. Building `/basketball/market` today produces a page that is byte-identical to `/nba-top-shot/market` plus a duplicate-content penalty, and it costs an audit of every single-collection assumption in the read layer. Build the aggregate lens (Option C), and promote to A the day a hobby has a second real collection.

**2. Do not chase the remaining FMV coverage percentages, and specifically do not auto-fill UFC.**
UFC sits at 89.8% coverage / 28.6% priced because the market migrated to Aptos on 05-13 and is dead. Manufacturing prices for a dead market is the fabricated-data class — the same class as the `$0.00` realized pull value and the 515 mis-tiered editions that were fixed today. The correct product answer is a label ("this market has been inactive since May 2026"), not a number. Same for the 79 unpriced Candy editions and the 60% of Panini that discovery cannot reach.

**3. Do not build more `/insights` boards.**
There are 26 cards on that hub and the single best one is at position #24. Adding a 27th does not solve a discovery problem; it deepens it. Until `/insights/account-value` is #1 and converting, board count is negative-value work.

**4. Do not gate anything back behind sign-in to "drive signups."**
It will work — briefly and catastrophically. The 07-17 un-gate was correct, and the 0-signup number is not caused by content being too free; it is caused by the account having no job (§0.1). Re-gating trades the one asset we have (a fully public, crawlable, linkable product) for a metric that would go up because the denominator collapsed.

**5. Do not deploy the Trade Hub, even to testnet, as part of "finishing" it.**
The 5 FCL submitters are wired for real and unverified on-chain. Under Trevor's read-only direction the correct action is deletion. Finishing a surface we have decided to remove is the most expensive possible way to reduce it to zero.

**6. Do not build more test coverage as a default activity.**
8,504 tests, 66 pinned DB invariants, both vitest gates saturated, a rendered-DOM monitor, and a drift guard. Multiple recent sessions concluded independently that the remaining candidates are coverage theater. The next test worth writing is one that pins a *specific* thing we are about to change — the `fcl.mutate`-call-sites-must-be-zero check in §7.1 is a good example. Aggregate coverage as a goal is done.
