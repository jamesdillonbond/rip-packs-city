## 1. The verdict, up front

**The platform is materially safer, more correct, and better monitored than it was 48 hours ago. None of that has moved the only number that matters, and none of it will.**

The audit push closed a live credential exposure, revoked anonymous read access to 793,152 rows of cost-basis data, closed five IDOR routes, deleted every transactional write surface from the frontend, un-broke two public pages that were serving wrong or failed answers, more than doubled the pack-EV surface, made the monitoring system able to read itself again, and took the trust-health board from 25 arms to 32. That is genuinely excellent engineering.

It is also, with respect to traction, worth exactly zero. Here is the live read:

- **0 signups in the last 7 days. 0 in the last 30. The last account was created 2026-06-10 — 53 days ago.**
- **20 users, lifetime. 0 email subscribers.**
- 473 sessions in 30 days, of which **397 (84%) fired exactly one event** and only **37 (7.8%) carried a referrer at all**.
- **25 wallet pastes lifetime, 4 in 30 days, 1 in the last 7** (2026-08-01 19:59 UTC).

Every hour spent on correctness in the last two days was spent making a product nobody has seen more trustworthy for the nobody who is seeing it. That is not an argument against having done it — a product that lies is not a product, and several of the things fixed were the kind that get you called wrong in public the first time someone real arrives. It *is* an argument against doing another two days of it before Phase 0 produces a non-zero number.

**The strategy is unchanged: this is a distribution problem. Phase 0 remains worth more than Phases 1–4 combined.**

## 4. The metric that matters — unchanged

**Weekly active users, where active means a human session firing ≥2 events, one of which is a wallet paste or a saved-wallet read.** Bar unchanged: **50 WAU unlocks monetization; nothing before it does.**

Secondary, and the leading indicator: the **absolute count** of `wallet_paste` events, not a rate. Currently 25 / 4 / 1 (lifetime / 30d / 7d). Any week with a paste from a session that is not Trevor's is new information.

**Falsification conditions, unchanged — written down so we cannot retroactively decide we were right:**

1. 500 real (referrer-bearing, multi-event) sessions reach `/insights/account-value` and paste conversion stays under ~3% → the wedge does not land; the answer is a different product, not a bigger megaphone.
2. Pastes happen but return-within-7-days is under ~10% → valuation is a one-time curiosity; pivot to alerting/watching.
3. Someone else ships dual-scale multi-collection valuation and it does not move their numbers either → the wedge is not a wedge.

## 5. Phase 0 — Distribution & Activation

**Unchanged in substance.** Restated because it is still the whole game.

### 0.1 The account has no job — give it one *(highest leverage item in this document)*

Signing up buys a bookmark. That explains 0 signups in 53 days better than any funnel theory. The fix is to make the account watch the market while the user sleeps: save a wallet → weekly or on-threshold "your collection moved $X" email; save a wallet → alert when an edition you own crosses a threshold. The `weekly-digest` send loop exists and is tested; `email_subscribers` is 0 because nothing ever asks. The CTA stops being "create an account" and becomes **"we'll tell you when your collection changes."**

It is the direct answer to the question the company is built around and it sits 24th of 26 cards on the `/insights` hub. Make it card #1. Link it from the homepage above the fold.

The `<h1>` rewrite landed in the 08-01 wave. Leave it; the SEO tail is months long and there is nothing to measure yet.

### 0.4 ~~Fix the wallet-box leak~~ — **CLOSED BY MEASUREMENT. Do not reopen.**

The 441-view → 0-paste reading was a **traffic** reading, not a conversion reading. Re-verified today: of 473 sessions in 30 days, 397 fired exactly one event and only 37 carried a referrer. The realistic human denominator is ~49 engaged sessions, and **3 of those 49 pasted a wallet (6.1%)** — an unremarkable rate on a sample far too small to conclude anything from.

## 6. Phase 1 — Dual-scale "what is my collection worth"

**Unchanged.** The wedge is not valuation, it is **dual-scale** valuation: (a) everything across all collections and all chains in one number, and (b) inside one collection across both items *and* packs. The packs half of (b) is the differentiated piece and the half most likely to be quietly wrong.

What has to be true is unchanged from 08-01. What has changed is that the data table in §3 is more honest than the one that roadmap published, and it makes the required disclosure sharper:

- **A rollup must report "priced", not "covered."** 99.3% Top Shot coverage and 82.0% Top Shot priced are different promises. Publish the second.
- **A rollup must age its inputs.** 32.2% of priced Top Shot FMVs and 96.1% of priced UFC FMVs are over 30 days old. A total assembled from those is not wrong so much as undated.
- **A rollup must separate asks from sales.** 18.7% of Flow prices are `ASK_ONLY`.

Deliverable is unchanged — one page, the promoted `/insights/account-value`:

```
Your collection:            $X,XXX
  NBA Top Shot   items $A + packs $B
  NFL All Day    items $C + packs $D
  Disney Pinnacle items $E + packs $F
  …
  Not priced: 214 Panini cards (listing-gated), 12 UFC packs (no EV model)
  3,530 Top Shot editions priced from asks, not sales
```

The "Not priced" and "priced from asks" lines are the thing that makes the rest of the number trustworthy, and they are the sentences no competitor writes.

## 9. Phase 4 — Read-only conversion + the wallet/identity model

### ▲ 9.1 The read-only strip is DONE — this half of the phase is closed

Cart, gifting, and the Trade Hub are deleted from the tree (55 files), including the unverified live-fire mainnet-signing code. The Cadence contract source and its green 16/16 `flow test` suite were deliberately kept; the DB tables were untouched; `git revert <sha>` restores the whole feature if it is ever wanted.

**One item remains from 9.1:** make it mechanical. There should be **zero `fcl.mutate` call sites** in the tree, enforced by a test. That test does not exist yet and is the correct kind of test to write — it pins a specific thing we have decided about, rather than chasing an aggregate coverage number.

The positioning payoff stands and should be used: **"we never touch your assets and we never ask you to sign a transaction"** is a trust asset in a hobby full of people who have been rugged, not a footnote.

### 9.2 Replace the `owner_key` identity model — **this is what Phase 4 now is**

Unchanged and still the most important structural finding of the audit: **`owner_key` is polymorphic and client-controlled** — it can be an auth UUID, a `profile_bio.username`, or a `0x` address, **with no server-side mapping table.** That is why five routes were IDOR-able. `lib/auth/owner-key-guard.ts` stops the bleeding; it does not fix the model.

Trevor's wallet model is the replacement and is a better model than what exists:

> Users register the wallets they want to track on their RPC profile — **one per chain**, except Flow, which allows **three** (Dapper, Flow Wallet, Flow EVM).

```
user_wallets (
  user_id      uuid       not null references auth.users,
  chain        chain_type not null,
  wallet_kind  text       not null,   -- 'dapper' | 'flow_wallet' | 'flow_evm' | 'primary'
  address      text       not null,
  label        text,
  verified_at  timestamptz,
  primary key (user_id, chain, wallet_kind)
)
```

- The composite PK **enforces one-per-chain / three-for-Flow structurally**, not in application code.
- RLS: a user reads and writes only their own rows. Server-side writes resolve `user_id` from **the session**, never from a request body. That single rule kills the IDOR class permanently.
- Every `owner_key` consumer migrates to `user_id`.

This is the debt repayment that unlocks safe personalization — which is what Phase 0.1 (give the account a job) needs in order to be built on something other than sand.

### 9.3 Sign-in — unchanged

Magic link stays the default (front door open since 07-20, allow-by-default minus `deny_list`). The listing challenge is built and live but far too heavy for signup — correct as an optional verified-badge upgrade. "Sign in with Dapper" is the best option for our exact audience and is still blocked on developer access; worth chasing, not worth blocking on.

**Recommendation unchanged:** make the account a side effect of getting something valuable — "paste a wallet → we'll email you when it moves → confirm your email."

### 9.4 What must never be exposed

Unchanged, and three of these were found live in the audit:

1. **Session tokens and credentials.** HEAD is clean; **git history is not** — see §10.1.
2. **Cost basis tied to an identity.** On-chain says a wallet paid $40. It does not say whose wallet it is. `moment_acquisitions` (793,152 rows) was anon-readable; joined to a named profile that is a stranger's realized P&L with their name on it. Revoked — verified still revoked today.
3. **Email addresses**, and anything mapping an email to a wallet.
4. **Any write path to another user's data.** Permanent fix is §9.2.
5. **Watchlists and alert configurations.** A watchlist is a statement of intent — it tells a competitor what a user is about to buy. Revoked; verified.

**The durable rule, now learned three times in this codebase:** route-gating is not data-gating. `proxy.ts` gates HTTP routes; a table with the default Supabase `anon` grant is readable at `/rest/v1/<table>` regardless. Every new table needs an explicit `REVOKE SELECT … FROM anon, authenticated`, verified with `has_table_privilege` — never by reading `information_schema`.

### 9.5 Concierge hardening — unchanged

No write tools that resolve identity from anything the model can be talked into passing. Multi-wallet awareness (the Phase-1 promise expressed conversationally, and the best demo of the wedge we have). Mirrored to Discord and Telegram — that is a **Phase 0/1 distribution item**, not a Phase 4 deferral: a bot answering "what's this moment worth" inside the Discord where collectors already are is a lower-friction front door than any webpage.

### 10.4 `unmapped_resolution_backlog_max` = 100, breaching — an honest open finding

Verified breaching at exactly its threshold. ### 10.5 ▲ `players` duplicates — the fix works, and there are **two more factories**

The 08-02 dedupe took Top Shot from 4,433 to 1,359 rows with 0 duplicate slugs, and `resolve_canonical_player` closed the `wallet-search` factory. Live today: **1,384 rows, 25 duplicate slugs.** Three findings:

1. **The primary fix works.** 52 `flow:<playID>` rows were minted at 12:49 UTC — in the **~9-minute window between the migration landing (12:40:38 UTC) and the code deploy**. **Zero `flow:` rows have been created since 13:00 UTC.** One more dedupe pass clears the residue.
2. **▲ A second, ongoing factory exists that the handoff never named.** `ensure_players_from_edition_names` mints `<collection_slug>-<name-slug>` and `app/api/ingest/route.ts` mints `<nba_stats_id>` — **two schemes for the same human, neither aware of the other.** Verified concrete case: `John Havlicek` exists twice — `nba_top_shot-john-havlicek` created 2026-08-02 01:22 UTC and `76970` created 2026-08-02 15:35 UTC, **after** the dedupe. This will keep producing duplicate slugs indefinitely. Both writers should route through `resolve_canonical_player`.
3. **The `ON CONFLICT` hazard is still live.** Confirmed by reading the file: `app/api/ingest/route.ts:321` still upserts players with `onConflict: "external_id,collection_id"` while `players` carries a **stricter global `UNIQUE(external_id)`**. The composite does not arbitrate the bare constraint, so a collision raises a unique violation rather than doing nothing.

### 10.6 ▲ Accented player slugs — bigger than one player

`Luka Dončić` slugs to `luka-don-i-`, so `/player/luka-doncic` 404s. **Live count: 191 player rows whose slug differs from its `unaccent`ed form** — this is not one edge case, it is ~14% of the Top Shot roster and it disproportionately hits European and Latin American players. `unaccent` is installed.

Still needs its own pass, because the expression is shared by `get_player_detail`, `ensure_players_from_edition_names`, and the dedupe migration, and changing it **moves live player URLs**. Do it with a redirect plan, not as a drive-by.

## 11. What this audit push proved about process

**Roughly eight separate premises were disproved on contact with the system.** Not eight bugs — eight *stated conclusions*, several of them written into handoffs and acted on as fact. Enumerated because the pattern is the lesson:

- A "dead" table (`special_serial_holders`, 25 rows, no page reads it) turned out to be a `UNION ALL` arm feeding a **live public board**. A `DROP` would have blanked it.
- A component-coverage "dip" that reverted a legitimate deletion turned out to be **one stale test assertion**, not a coverage regression. A scoped coverage run had been read as a global ratchet reading.
- A "duplicate" cron scheduled to be disabled turned out to be **the only one firing** — its supposed sibling has never run once.
- A "441 views → 0 pastes" conversion crisis turned out to be a **traffic reading**; the real denominator was ~8 human sessions.
- A "force-push incident" turned out to be a **split git remote** in a sandbox, where `origin` fetches from a local mount and pushes to GitHub. Nothing was ever lost.
- A view profiled at 122 ms turned out to be **7,722 ms** — the probe selected two columns, the planner elided the expensive join, and the real caller selects thirteen.
- A "toolchain conflict" blocking `edge-deno` for weeks turned out to be a **missing `--config` flag**; the prescribed fix (a `jsr:`→`npm:` remap) was unnecessary deploy-affecting churn.
- A handoff's proposed 2-row `UPDATE` of `collection_id` **would have failed** on a unique constraint the handoff did not know about.

**Three durable rules follow, and they should govern how the next large change is validated:**

1. **Distrust the instrument before the system.** Half the false conclusions above came from a measurement taken the wrong way — a scoped coverage run, a narrowed `EXPLAIN` projection, a 73-hour-retention table read as history, a git remote nobody checked. *Before* concluding a system is broken, prove the thing measuring it is not.
2. **A premise inherited from a handoff is a hypothesis, not a fact.** Every one of the eight was written down confidently by a prior session. The sessions that got value out of them got it by **re-measuring first** — and in at least four cases the entire value of the session was in *not* doing what was asked.
3. **Sweep `pg_proc` bodies and dependent views, not just a code grep, before deleting anything.** Two near-misses this week (`special_serial_holders`, the `players` tie-break that was silently load-bearing on the duplicates being deleted) came from the same shape: correctness that depends on redundancy rather than on the data being right.

**What this implies for the next big change** — the `user_wallets` migration in §9.2 is the next one that touches identity across dozens of consumers. It should be validated the way the good sessions here were: enumerate every consumer from `pg_proc` and the route tree before touching anything, verify each claimed consumer live rather than from the plan, and land it in reversible slices with a measured before/after on each — not as one migration justified by a document.

## 13. What I am deliberately NOT recommending, and why

**1. Do not run another correctness/audit pass before Phase 0 produces a non-zero number.**
This is the new one, and it is the most important item in this section. The last two days closed real, serious problems — but the marginal defect now being found is a UFC page labelled "Flow", a tier stripe that is the wrong orange, and a slug alias that self-canonicalizes. Those are real and they are worth ~nothing against 0 signups in 53 days. The correctness bar has been cleared. Ship the open items in §10 that are *risk* (10.1 credential purge, 10.5 identity factory, 10.2/10.3 pipeline health) and stop.

**2. Do not build the Hobby axis as a full six-section parallel app (Option A) yet — but the trigger is closer than we thought.**
Basketball still contains exactly one collection, so `/basketball/market` today is a byte-identical duplicate of `/nba-top-shot/market` plus a duplicate-content penalty. **Soccer, however, already has two collections across two chains** and is the correct first Option-C page. Build the aggregate lens there; promote to A when a hobby genuinely needs it.

**3. Do not chase remaining FMV coverage percentages, and specifically do not auto-fill UFC.**
UFC sits at 28.8% priced with 96.1% of those prices over 30 days old because the market migrated to Aptos on 2026-05-13 and is dead. Manufacturing prices for a dead market is the fabricated-data class — the same class as the `$0.00` realized pull value fixed this week. The correct product answer is a label ("this market has been inactive since May 2026"), not a number. Same for the unpriced Candy editions and the ~61% of Panini that listing-gated discovery cannot reach.

**4. Do not build more `/insights` boards.**
26 cards on that hub, and the single best one sits at #24. A 27th deepens a discovery problem rather than solving one. Until `/insights/account-value` is #1 and converting, board count is negative-value work.

**5. Do not gate anything back behind sign-in to "drive signups."**
It will work briefly and catastrophically. The 07-17 un-gate was correct. Zero signups is not caused by content being too free — it is caused by the account having no job (§5.1). Re-gating trades the one asset we have, a fully public crawlable linkable product, for a metric that improves because the denominator collapsed.

**6. Do not deploy the Trade Hub, even to testnet.**
It is deleted, correctly. Finishing a surface we have decided to remove is the most expensive possible way to reduce it to zero. The contract source and its 16/16 test suite are retained; that is the right amount of preservation.

**7. Do not write more aggregate test coverage.**
Both vitest gates are saturated, 91 DB invariants are pinned and audited 89/89 clean against live prod, the extraction lever from `app/**` into `lib/**` is documented as exhausted, `edge-deno` is now blocking at 0 errors, and DB-pin staleness now enforces in CI. Multiple independent sessions have reached this conclusion. The next test worth writing pins a **specific** thing we are about to change — the "zero `fcl.mutate` call sites" check in §9.1 is exactly the right shape.

**8. Do not raise a threshold to clear a breaching monitor.**
Both current breaches are honest. `unmapped_resolution_backlog_max` needs a failure-reason exclusion, not a higher `breach_at`. `public_board_slow_count` needs nothing — it self-clears. Raising either is guard-defeat, and the audit already caught one instance of a liveness cap having been raised twice where the query should have been fixed instead.
