# Rip Packs City — Roadmap, 2026-08-03

**Supersedes `docs/strategy/roadmap-2026-08-02.md`, and reverses its spine.**

The 08-02 roadmap opened with "this is a distribution problem" and made Phase 0 — distribution and activation — worth more than everything else combined. **Trevor has rejected that framing outright:**

> "Our website has been so bad, it makes no sense to promote, nor for any user to ever come back. Until we hit 100% accuracy on things like FMV coverage (and getting it accurate), Pack EV is 100% accurate based upon what's still unopened in packs, sales history and inventories are accurate, etc, we should expect our site to have 0 users — it brings them no value vs the sites they already use."

That is not a re-prioritisation of the same list. It is a different theory of why the number is zero, and it is the better theory. The distribution thesis said: the product is good, nobody has seen it. The accuracy thesis says: anyone who saw it had no reason to come back, and pushing traffic at it converts a quiet zero into a public one.

**So: accuracy is not a phase. It is the gate. Zero users is the correct and expected state until the data beats the incumbents.** Every section below is sequenced behind that, and the reasons are given rather than asserted.

All figures re-queried live against prod on **2026-08-03 ~03:50 UTC (2026-08-02 ~20:50 PT)** unless marked otherwise. Nothing is transcribed from the previous roadmap; several of its figures did not survive re-measurement and are corrected in place.

## 1. The gate

**RPC ships when a collector can check any number on the site against the marketplace they already use and find it right.** Not "mostly right", not "right on Top Shot". Right, or explicitly labelled as not known.

That is a harder bar than it sounds, because the failure mode is not a blank cell — it is a **confident wrong number**. A blank teaches a user the tool has limits. A wrong FMV, a pack EV computed against a pool that was emptied a year ago, a wallet total missing a whole collection — each of those teaches a user the tool cannot be trusted, and that lesson is permanent and free to spread.

Three consequences that this document is built on:

1. **Zero users is not a symptom to fix. It is the correct output of the current input.** Do not spend effort explaining it, instrumenting it, or optimising the funnel that feeds it.
2. **Traffic is a multiplier on whatever the product currently is.** At the present accuracy it multiplies a negative. Distribution is not merely lower priority than accuracy — before the gate it is *actively harmful*, which is why every growth tactic has been removed from this document rather than demoted.
3. **A number we cannot stand behind must not render as a number.** "—" with a reason is a shipped feature. This is the one rule that lets us clear the gate without first fixing every upstream market.

### What "100% accurate" resolves to, per Trevor's list

Trevor named four things. Each needs an operational definition, because "100%" against a partly-dead, partly-unindexed set of markets is otherwise unachievable and therefore unmanageable:

| Trevor's phrase | The bar, operationally |
|---|---|
| **FMV coverage, and getting it accurate** | Every edition either carries a price a collector can reconcile against the market — published FMV within a defensible band of that edition's own realised sales — or renders `—` with a stated reason. **No edition may carry a price derived from a market that is closed.** |
| **Pack EV is 100% accurate based on what's still unopened** | EV is computed against the **remaining** pool, not the original print run, or it is labelled as an original-supply estimate and excluded from anything that looks like a buy signal. A pack nobody can buy does not publish an actionable-looking ratio. |
| **Sales history is accurate** | Within the indexed window, every sale we show is a real sale with correct edition, serial, price and counterparties — and where the window does not cover the market's history, the surface says so rather than implying the total is a census. |
| **Inventories are accurate** | A pasted wallet returns every item it holds, in every collection, with metadata and a price — or names the collection it cannot price. A wallet total that silently omits a collection is the single worst defect class we have, because it is invisible and wrong in the user's favour. |

### 2.1 The `$0.50` dust floor is removed — the platform's largest single accuracy defect

`DUST_PRICE_USD = 0.5` ran as step 1 of `dampenGrailSpike()`, discarding every sale under fifty cents **before any pricing math**. It discarded **46% of Top Shot and 76% of All Day** 30-day transactions — the real bottom of both order books, not noise (1,094 distinct TS buyers below $0.50 in 30 days, zero self-trades, a price histogram continuous through the cut whose mode is the $0.25 marketplace minimum). Because survivors are the top of the distribution, published FMV landed above the market. It was never derived from data: no ledger entry, no test, no audit behind the constant.

**The decision was made on a production control group, not a model.** `drain_fmv_cold_tail()` already prices Top Shot with a plain 30-day median and *no* dust floor — same collection, same window, same editions. That is a genuine A/B that was running in production the whole time:

## 4. The work, in order

Ordered by how much each unblocks a collector trusting a number. Not by effort, and deliberately not by which pipeline is most interesting.

**Gate 1 — nothing can render a number we know is wrong.** This is cheap, it is mostly labelling, and it clears the "actively harmful" property in §1 without fixing a single upstream market.

1. **Finish the dust-floor verification (§5.1).** Everything below assumes FMV is now approximately right; confirm it before building on it.
2. **Denormalise Candy FMV into `wallet_moments_cache`.** A wallet total that silently omits a fully-priced collection is the worst defect we have and the cheapest to fix.
3. **Drain the 4,249 Golazos shells**, and until they are gone, have any wallet total that includes Golazos state how many rows it could not price.
4. **Label every dead-market surface.** UFC prices carry a closure notice wherever they render, not only in the schema.org payload. A UFC wallet does not show a total; it shows a count and a note.
5. **Pack EV must not present a retired pack's ratio in the same visual language as an actionable one.** 108 of 4,596 is the number to design around.

**Gate 2 — make the prices defensible.** This is the actual product work and it is where the months are.

6. **All Day FMV: 6.3% → the Top Shot band or better.** A collection with 17,240 sales in 30 days has no excuse for a 6.3% sales-backed rate. Diagnose the confidence pipeline, not the market.
7. **Move All Day and Golazos pack EV to a remaining-pool basis**, or stop publishing an EV number for them and publish the pool composition instead. 89,783 never-decremented rows is a data-model gap, not a tuning knob.
8. **Top Shot: 19.8% of priced editions are over 30 days stale.** Decide per edition whether stale means "price it from asks and say so" or "render `—`". Do not leave a 30-day-old sale rendering as a current price.
9. **Golazos: accept that ~99% of the catalogue cannot be priced from sales** and design the surface for that truth rather than filling it with asks.

**Gate 3 — completeness.** Only once the published numbers are defensible.

10. Close the remaining ingest gaps that cap coverage (Panini listing-gated discovery, Pinnacle's render-keyed plane, the counterparty tail).

**Only after Gate 2 does anything in §5 start.**

### 5.2 `pinnacle-sync` observability — fixed this session

The permanent false stall (`detect_stalled_pipelines` reporting ~2,496 min silent against a 1,560 min threshold, paging Telegram repeatedly) was **not** a missing `log_pipeline_run` and **not** a dead pipeline. The route has logged since PIN-SYNC-OBS — but only from **inside `after()`**, so when Vercel drops or freezes the deferred work the run leaves no row at all while the work completes. Proven: on 2026-08-02 the DB function `pinnacle_fmv_recalc_render_all` self-logged `pinnacle-fmv-recalc` at 10:07:13Z with 2,160 renders priced, while `pinnacle-sync` logged nothing.

Fixed by writing a synchronous `phase: "invoked"` marker before `after()` is scheduled, plus a fatal-catch, so "after() dropped" and "never invoked" are distinguishable. **Also removed the duplicate Vercel cron entry** (`0 6 * * *`), which had never once executed the route body — Vercel Cron can only send `CRON_SECRET` and this route accepts only `INGEST_SECRET_TOKEN`. ⚠ **Note for whoever watches this next:** `pinnacle-sync`'s watchlist threshold is 1,560 min (26h) for a job that runs once every 24h — two hours of slack. The invocation marker makes the signal reliable, but a single dropped cron-job.org tick will still alarm. That is arguably correct; do not widen it to silence a real dropout.

## 6. What comes after the gate — kept, and explicitly sequenced behind it

These are retained from the 08-02 roadmap because they are right. They are sequenced **after** Gate 2, and the reason is the same in every case: **a feature built on inaccurate inputs is a more efficient way to publish a wrong number.**

### 6.1 Dual-scale valuation — the wedge, still

(a) Everything the user owns across all collections and all chains in one number, and (b) inside one collection, items **and** unopened packs. The packs half is the differentiated piece.

**Why it waits:** a cross-wallet total is a *product* of its inputs. Today it would silently drop Candy entirely (100% of Candy wallet rows have no FMV), under-count Golazos by 44.7%, and fold in UFC values from a market that has been closed for 81 days. A collector who checks that total against their own records finds it wrong and never returns — and unlike a wrong edition price, a wrong total is un-auditable by the user, so they cannot even tell us which part broke. **A confidently wrong aggregate is worse than no aggregate**, because it fails silently and it fails at exactly the moment we asked for trust.

When it ships, the disclosure lines are part of the feature, not decoration:

```
Your collection:            $X,XXX
  NBA Top Shot   items $A + packs $B
  NFL All Day    items $C + packs $D
  ...
  Not priced: 214 Panini cards (listing-gated), 12 UFC packs (no EV model)
  3,530 Top Shot editions priced from asks, not sales
```

### 6.2 Six-section parity + IA restructure

My Wallet / Market / Sniper / Play / Sets / Analytics, with Overview folded into Analytics. Parity is what makes the §6.1 promise true in every collection rather than just Top Shot. Where a collection's data cannot support a section, say so on the page; never ship an empty tab.

**Why it waits:** parity multiplies whatever accuracy each collection has. Bringing All Day to full section parity at 6.3% sales-backed FMV builds six surfaces that are each wrong in the same way.

### 6.3 The Hobby axis

Ship **Option C** (`/hobby/<name>` as an aggregate lens; collections remain the workspace). Not Option A (route prefix — highest cost, duplicate content where a hobby has one collection), never Option B (query param — gains nothing on discovery).

**Start with soccer**, the only hobby that is already two collections across two chains (LaLiga Golazos on Flow + Panini on Ethereum) and therefore the only one where Option C is not a byte-identical duplicate of an existing collection page. Caveat unchanged: `panini_blockchain` is unpublished, so including it is a publication decision — Trevor's call.

**Why it waits:** a hobby page is an aggregate, so §6.1's reasoning applies with more force — it aggregates across *chains*, where the coverage asymmetry is largest.

### 6.4 `owner_key` → real user↔wallet model

Unchanged and still the most important structural finding of the audit: **`owner_key` is polymorphic and client-controlled** — an auth UUID, a `profile_bio.username`, or a `0x` address, with no server-side mapping table. That is why five routes were IDOR-able; `lib/auth/owner-key-guard.ts` stops the bleeding without fixing the model.

The replacement is Trevor's model: users register the wallets they track on their profile — **one per chain, except Flow which allows three** (Dapper, Flow Wallet, Flow EVM).

```
user_wallets (
  user_id      uuid       not null references auth.users,
  chain        chain_type not null,
  wallet_kind  text       not null,   -- 'dapper' | 'flow_wallet' | 'flow_evm' | 'primary'
  address      text       not null,
  label        text,
  verified_at  timestamptz,
  primary key (user_id, chain, wallet_kind)
)
```

The composite PK enforces one-per-chain / three-for-Flow **structurally**. RLS restricts a user to their own rows, and server-side writes resolve `user_id` from **the session, never from a request body** — that single rule kills the IDOR class permanently.

**This one may proceed in parallel with Gate 2**, because it is a security and correctness repayment rather than a user-facing number, and because §6.1 cannot be built safely on top of the current model regardless.

## 7. What this thread proved about process

Roughly ten stated conclusions were disproved on contact with the system — not ten bugs, ten *premises*, several written into handoffs and acted on as fact:

- A "dead" table (`special_serial_holders`) was a `UNION ALL` arm feeding a live public board. A `DROP` would have blanked it.
- A component-coverage "dip" was one stale test assertion; a scoped run had been read as a global ratchet.
- A "duplicate" cron scheduled for disabling was the **only one firing** — and re-checked this session, its Vercel sibling has never once executed the route body.
- A "441 views → 0 pastes" conversion crisis was a **traffic** reading; the real human denominator was ~8.
- A view profiled at 122 ms was 7,722 ms — the probe selected two columns and the planner elided the expensive join.
- A weeks-long `edge-deno` "toolchain conflict" was a missing `--config` flag.
- A handoff's 2-row `UPDATE` would have failed on a unique constraint the handoff did not know about.
- A VACUUM was proposed for a table already **95.7% all-visible**, where it could not have helped.
- `is_serialized` was assumed to mean serialized; 188 Open Editions claim it and are not.
- And this session: "`pinnacle-sync` never calls `log_pipeline_run`" — it has since PIN-SYNC-OBS. The real defect was narrower and more interesting: it logs only from *inside* `after()`.

**These are not ten unrelated mistakes. Every one is the same mistake: a conclusion about a system, drawn from an instrument nobody validated first.** A 73-hour-retention table read as history. A narrowed `EXPLAIN`. A scoped coverage run. A boolean column trusted because of its name. A cron assumed to fire because it is scheduled.

**The rule that follows — apply it before acting on any premise, including one in this document:**

> **Validate the instrument before the system, and state the measurement rather than the conclusion.**

Operationally, three checks, in order:

1. **Re-measure at the source of truth, not the summary.** `pipeline_runs_daily`, not `pipeline_runs`. Live `pg_proc.prosrc`, not the migration file. The full projection the real caller uses, not a two-column probe. A rendered DOM, not an HTTP 200.
2. **Before reading a zero as absence, prove the instrument covers the thing.** Retention window, scope, filter, grant, and whether the metric key exists at all — `sum((extra->>'k')::int)` over a missing key is NULL and reads as 0.
3. **Before deleting or changing something, prove it is load-bearing by breaking it deliberately** in a scratch copy and watching a specific thing fail. If nothing fails, you do not yet understand it well enough to change it. This is the same discipline as mutation-testing a DB pin, applied to prose claims.

**And the corollary that earned its keep repeatedly: in at least four of the ten cases, the entire value of the session was in *not* doing what was asked.** A premise inherited from a handoff is a hypothesis. Re-measuring first is not diligence overhead — it is frequently the whole job.

**Applied to what is next:** §5.1 (the dust-floor re-baseline) is itself a premise awaiting measurement — it is 48 minutes old and confirmed only in direction. And the `user_wallets` migration in §6.4 touches identity across dozens of consumers: enumerate every consumer from `pg_proc` and the route tree before touching anything, verify each claimed consumer live rather than from the plan, and land it in reversible slices with a measured before/after — not as one migration justified by a document.
