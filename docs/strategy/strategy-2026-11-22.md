### Drop week is where pricing was worst, and it is when collectors look hardest

The 11-11 Rookie Debut / Series 9 launch was the worst accuracy event on record for Top Shot, and nothing alarmed:

- **On 11-11, 37.6 % of Top Shot sales went through while the live price was ASK_ONLY.** Within-±25 % at sale fell from ~79 % to **47.8 %**. On 11-12 the ASK_ONLY share was 16.1 % and within-±25 % was 65.1 %.
- On Series 9 that week, **2,399 sales sold against an ASK_ONLY price at a median 2.16× the sale price**, within ±25 % only 8.8 %. That was measured at **lag 0**, the price live at the moment of sale, so it is not an artefact of the backtest's one-day lag.
- **Mechanism, read on one Rookie Debut edition:** one snapshot at 1:09 PM PT 11-11 ($0.33 ASK_ONLY), then **no new snapshot for ~20 hours while 37 sales cleared at ~$0.18**. The model was fine; repricing was too slow. When it did reprice, it read $0.18 MEDIUM.
- **The fix may already exist.** The fmv-recalc **hot lane** shipped 11-12 (`HOT_LANE_LIMIT 150`, `app/api/fmv-recalc/route.ts`). It reprices editions that sold since their last snapshot and lack sale-backed depth, which is exactly the drop-day population. Since 11-14, Series 9 ASK_ONLY at sale is down to 79 sales (ratio 1.64), and Top Shot overall reads ~80 % within ±25 % at sale. But the hot lane **has not faced a drop yet**: it handles ~7 editions per run today against a cap of 150. Rookie Debut alone is 152 editions.
- The weekly accuracy job will show it 11-23 (the 11-16 week). The 11-09 row already stores the failure as ASK_ONLY within-±25 % **18.4 %**, so the instrument saw it a week late, by design.
