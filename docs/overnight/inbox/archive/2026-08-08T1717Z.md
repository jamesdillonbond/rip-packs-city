## The two findings

**1. Every output is tiny; the cost is entirely in the defining query.** 163 rows → 128 kB up to 19k rows → 1.5 MB, yet refreshes cost up to 600s. This is the exact class the perfect-mint-premiums ledger entry named: a query-cost problem, NOT a data-volume or cadence problem. The durable fix per job is to read its defining query and add the missing index (same shape as the `get_team_detail` slug-scan and `idx_editions_collection_team_slug` fixes) — near-certainly an unindexed scan/median over `sales`/serial data. **Do NOT bump `statement_timeout`** (documented anti-fix — the sentinel fails blind on a longer ceiling).

**2. Scheduling contention amplifies it — this is partly a stagger problem, not only a per-query problem.** Two jobs (`set-play-catalog` avg 43.9s, `pinnacle-acquisitions` avg 78.6s) are NOT inherently too slow; they only fail when they spike into contention. Note the pileups:
- **:17 every hour** — `perfect-mint-premiums` (hourly) collides at :17 of every 6h with `pinnacle-acquisitions` (`17 */6`) and at :17 of every 3h with `set-play-catalog` (`17 */3`).
- **The 6-hourly heavy window** — `edition-median` (`10 */6`), `allday-pack-sales-agg` (`20 */6`), `allday-pack-realized` (`35 */6`) all refresh heavy MVs within a 25-min band, on a pooler the ledger already measures as saturated (live PGRST003 on read-only queries). Simultaneous heavy `REFRESH ... CONCURRENTLY` on a saturated pool is how a normally-43s job hits its 120s ceiling.

## Candidate levers (for the night pass — NOT applied here)

- **Config-only, low-risk first pass: stagger** the `*/6` heavy refreshes off each other and off the :17 hourly/3h jobs (respect the `rpc-cron-ops` stagger discipline). This costs nothing in freshness and may clear the contention-driven failures on the jobs that aren't inherently slow (`set-play-catalog`, `pinnacle-acquisitions`, `allday-pack-sales-agg`). Reversible via `cron.schedule`.

Then `allday-pack-realized` / `allday-pack-sales-agg`.
